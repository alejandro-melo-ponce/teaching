# ECON547 — Advanced Game Theory: Mechanism Design

**Nazarbayev University · Graduate · Fall 2024 · Individualized research seminar**  
**Instructor:** Alejandro Melo Ponce

I ran this course as an individualized seminar, with the central aim of helping the enrolled students make substantial progress toward a research paper or MA thesis. I tailored the readings and discussions closely to the literature and theoretical tools they needed to develop their own ideas.

I used the syllabus as a flexible guide to possible topics. We covered some of those topics, but I adapted the seminar to the students' research questions as their projects developed. My priority was to help them move from an initial idea toward a clearly formulated question, a command of the relevant literature, and a model they could analyze.

## Research focus

I framed the seminar around mechanism design and advanced game theory. The syllabus offered starting points in mechanism design and implementation, incomplete information and robustness, information design, and mechanisms for boundedly rational agents. These provided a foundation for more focused engagement with the research literature relevant to each student's project.

I asked students to develop a research proposal, present their progress, and write a paper draft. I expected the draft to explain the question and its contribution, review the relevant literature, and set out the mathematical framework, including at least one fully solved example. I assigned 25% of the grade to the proposal, 25% to the presentation, and 50% to the paper draft.

# ECON403/503 — Introduction to Contract Theory and Auctions

**Nazarbayev University · Senior undergraduate and master's students**  
**Offerings:** Fall 2019, Fall 2020, Fall 2021, Fall 2022, Fall 2023, Spring 2025, and Fall 2025.

I developed this course in response to the needs of our students and the department's teaching provision. Its content evolved from information economics to advanced game theory and auctions. I preserve that development in this collection, while making the final consolidated materials easy to find.

## Three stages of the course

**2019 — Information economics.** I focused on choice under uncertainty and principal–agent models: hidden information and screening, and hidden action and moral hazard.

**2020–2021 — The pandemic-era revision.** I continued to teach the same information-economics topics, changing the approach and depth of coverage. Slides, handwritten board notes, and saved virtual-session work document this version of the course.

**2022 onward — Advanced game theory and auctions.** When Prof. Levent Koçkesen resumed teaching information economics in 2022, I shifted this course toward knowledge and beliefs, Bayesian games, and auction theory. The later materials develop complete-information and Bayesian auctions, revenue equivalence, the envelope theorem, reserve prices, and interdependent values, supported by mathematical and probability notes.

The **consolidated 2026 revision** is the final reference collection for the later period's lectures, supplementary notes, and problem lists. This is a revision date, not a separate teaching offering. I also retain earlier versions and saved annotations where they add to the collection.

## Acknowledgments

I am grateful to my colleague **Prof. Levent Koçkesen**, whose lecture notes on information economics were an important resource in developing my notes on screening and moral hazard in principal–agent models for the 2019–2021 offerings.

For the course from 2022 onward, I drew on **Omer Tamuz's game-theory lecture notes**, **Michael Maschler, Eilon Solan, and Shmuel Zamir's *Game Theory***, and **Vijay Krishna's *Auction Theory***. These works provided a foundation for the course's organization and influenced its style of exposition. The selection of topics, depth of coverage, and emphasis reflect my own approach to teaching the course.

The supplementary note giving a counterexample to the no-trade theorem is coauthored with **Abuzer Abuov**.

# ECON498 — Advanced Topics in Economics: General Equilibrium

**Nazarbayev University · Spring 2026**  
**Instructor:** Alejandro Melo Ponce

I designed this course to give advanced undergraduate students a mathematically rigorous, PhD-level introduction to general equilibrium theory. My emphasis was on formal reasoning, careful modeling, and understanding the arguments behind existence and welfare results.

## Course scope

I organized the syllabus around the following themes:

- **Mathematical foundations:** metric spaces and topology, convexity and aggregation, correspondences and hemicontinuity, maximum theorems, and fixed-point methods.
- **Exchange and production economies:** the Walrasian framework, demand and excess demand, competitive equilibrium, and equilibrium existence.
- **Welfare and efficiency:** Pareto efficiency, supporting prices, and the First and Second Welfare Theorems.
- **Cooperative foundations:** the core, blocking coalitions, and core convergence in large economies.
- **Indivisibilities and allocation:** economies with nonconvexities and indivisible goods, with connections to assignment problems and auctions.

I also included equilibrium under uncertainty and economies with a continuum of agents as possible extensions, time permitting. I expected students to be comfortable with mathematical reasoning, while developing the necessary analytical tools as part of the course.

## General-equilibrium lecture notes

I began writing these notes for ECON498, developing much of the mathematical material while I was teaching the course. They have since grown to book length and cover considerably more than I could teach in a single course. They remain lecture notes in purpose and style, and I do not intend to develop them for formal publication as a book.

My aim is to guide students through the material, encourage their interest in general equilibrium theory, and provide a main reference that they can return to during the course and in more advanced studies. The notes bring together material from many different sources, which I acknowledge throughout the document. I make no claim to originality in the underlying content; my contribution is in how I organize, explain, and connect it for students.

This is an ongoing teaching project, and I will continue to revise and expand the notes. I maintain them in a separate repository, where you can find the most recent released version:

**[General Equilibrium lecture notes — GE-Notes](https://github.com/alejandro-melo-ponce/ge-notes)**

## References

I used my own notes alongside the general equilibrium chapters of *Microeconomic Theory* by Mas-Colell, Whinston, and Green (1995), foundational work by Arrow, Debreu, and McKenzie, and mathematical references including Green and Heller, Rockafellar, Border, and Aliprantis and Border. Selected papers included Debreu and Scarf (1963), Anderson (1978), and Gul and Stacchetti (1999). Full references and the planned sequence are in the syllabus.

# ECON301 — Introduction to Econometrics

**Nazarbayev University · Undergraduate**  
**Offerings:** Spring 2020, Spring 2021, Spring 2022, and Spring 2023.

I taught this course to introduce students to empirical research and the statistical tools used to study economic relationships. I combined the theoretical foundations of econometrics with computational examples in R, problem sets, and an empirical research project. My aim was for students to understand both how to apply econometric methods and what assumptions make their conclusions meaningful.

## Course coverage

I begin with probability, conditional expectation, and functional relationships, then develop simple and multiple linear regression, the properties of least-squares estimators, hypothesis testing, and large-sample theory. Applications address qualitative variables, polynomial specifications, interactions, outliers, and multicollinearity. The course outline also allows for further topics, such as instrumental variables and time-series methods, depending on time and student interests.

The principal references are Jeffrey M. Wooldridge's *Introductory Econometrics: A Modern Approach* and Arthur S. Goldberger's *A First Course in Econometrics*. My notes develop the theory alongside examples and exercises; the R scripts connect that work to computation.

## Teaching materials and their development

The collection spans the transition to remote teaching in Spring 2020, the asynchronous offerings in Spring 2021 and Spring 2022, and my final offering in Spring 2023. I include the Spring 2020 COVID-19 syllabus update alongside the original syllabus.

The 2021 handwritten lecture series provides a shared foundation for the later offerings. I retain that series once and link to it from subsequent years. Saved group-office-hours and virtual-session notes preserve additional explanations and worked examples. In 2023, typeset versions complemented the handwritten notes, alongside annotated materials.

I organize lecture materials with preliminary notes first, followed by numbered lectures. Teaching scripts appear with the corresponding lecture where the connection is clear; general routines are grouped separately. Revised materials are retained, while identical files are linked across offerings.

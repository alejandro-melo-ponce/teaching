# ECON101 — Introduction to Microeconomics

**Nazarbayev University · Undergraduate**  
**Offerings:** Fall 2019, Fall 2020, Fall 2021, Spring 2023, Fall 2023, Fall 2024, and Spring 2026.

I taught this course as an introduction to economic reasoning: how individuals and firms make choices, how their decisions interact in markets, and how economic models help us evaluate public policy. Over successive offerings, I revised the readings, lecture materials, exercises, and assessments. This collection brings together the materials I share from those iterations.

## Course coverage

The course begins with opportunity cost, comparative advantage, supply and demand, and elasticity. From there, I develop consumer choice and demand, production and costs, firm behavior, and competitive equilibrium. Taxes, subsidies, and other government interventions provide applications throughout the course.

The later offerings give greater depth to consumer and producer theory, including preferences and budget sets, income and substitution effects, expenditure minimization, and the relationship between short-run and long-run costs. Depending on the offering, the course also extends to monopoly, strategic interaction, oligopoly, externalities, and public goods. The syllabus for each term provides the intended scope and assessment structure.

## How the course evolved

In the earlier offerings, I organized the course around a principal textbook: Acemoglu, Laibson, and List in 2019; Pindyck and Rubinfeld in 2020; Perloff in Fall 2021 and Spring 2023; and Frank, Bernanke, Antonovics, and Heffetz in Fall 2023.

By Fall 2024 and Spring 2026, I no longer assigned a single official textbook. I organized the course around my own materials, with selected references supporting a more detailed treatment of the theory. I retain materials by offering so readers can explore both the continuity and the changes across years.

During the pandemic-era offerings of Fall 2020 and Fall 2021, I taught asynchronously using recorded lectures and Moodle-based assessments. The collection includes teaching materials from those terms, but not their LMS assessment exports.

## Teaching approach

Whenever the classroom allowed it, I developed diagrams, explanations, and derivations live on a blackboard or through a digital blackboard and projector. I used this approach to engage students in working through the ideas during class. Auditorium settings sometimes limited how much board work I could incorporate.

The lecture collection includes slides, standalone handwritten board notes, and slides with saved annotations. Blank spaces often supported live work, and I did not save every explanation developed in class. The PDFs therefore accompany my teaching rather than provide a complete transcript of it. I include distinct saved annotation versions where they preserve different stages of that work.

The lecture notes shared here are my own materials from 2020 onward. I used textbook slides in 2019 and have not included those slides.

# Teaching Portfolio and Repository of Past Courses

**Alejandro Melo Ponce**

I share teaching materials from courses I have taught at Nazarbayev University and Stony Brook University, from introductory economics to advanced theory and research seminars. The collection includes lecture notes, saved board work, problem sets, computational examples, and retired examinations, as well as syllabi that document how I designed and revised the courses.

## How I teach

I emphasize economic reasoning: building a model, understanding its assumptions, and working through what follows from them. I connect formal arguments with intuition and applications, adapting the level of detail to the course and its students. In econometrics, I combine theory with computation in R and empirical research; in advanced theory courses, I develop the mathematical tools alongside the economic questions.

Whenever the classroom allows it, I work through diagrams, examples, and derivations live on a blackboard or a digital board and projector. I use this approach to engage students in developing the argument during class. The saved handwritten notes and annotations are part of that teaching. Some board work was not recorded, so the available PDFs do not capture every explanation developed in class.

I also adapt courses as students’ needs and the curriculum change. The archive preserves substantial revisions across offerings, including changes in depth, organization, and delivery during the pandemic. In research seminars, I tailor the readings and discussion to the questions students are developing.

## Assessment design

Designing new problems is a central part of my teaching. I usually write examinations anew for each offering, with occasional reuse or adaptation. I share retired exams both as resources for learning and as examples of my assessment design: how I ask students to reason through unfamiliar problems, connect ideas, and develop an argument.

Where available, I include worked solutions, grading rubrics, and answer booklets. The course pages identify materials developed with reference to textbooks or other instructors’ work.

## General-equilibrium lecture notes

My [general-equilibrium lecture notes](https://github.com/alejandro-melo-ponce/ge-notes) began with ECON498, and I wrote much of the mathematical material while teaching the course. They have since grown to book length and beyond the scope of that course, while retaining their purpose and character as lecture notes. I do not intend to develop them for formal publication as a book.

I write these notes to guide students, encourage their interest in the subject, and provide a main reference both for the course and for more advanced study. They draw on many sources acknowledged throughout the document; I make no claim to originality in the underlying content. I maintain the notes in their own repository and continue to revise and expand them.

## Using the collection

Start with a course README for its scope, teaching context, references, and material index. Within courses, I organize files by offering, topic, or stage of development, retaining meaningful revisions and linking shared copies where appropriate. The amount of material available differs by course; the indexes show the notes, exercises, solutions, and exams included.

# Teaching Portfolio and Repository of Past Courses

**Alejandro Melo Ponce**

This repository brings together teaching materials from courses I taught at Nazarbayev University and Stony Brook University, from introductory economics to advanced theory and research seminars. The collection spans eleven years of teaching (2015–2026), from my time as a graduate student instructor to my role as an assistant professor. It includes lecture notes, saved board work, problem sets, computational examples, and retired examinations, as well as syllabi that document how I designed and revised the courses.

## Teaching approach

In these courses, I emphasized economic reasoning: building a model, understanding its assumptions, and working through what follows from them. I connected formal arguments with intuition and applications, adapting the level of detail to the course and its students. In econometrics, I combined theory with computation in R and empirical research; in advanced theory courses, I developed the mathematical tools alongside the economic questions.

Whenever the classroom allowed it, I worked through diagrams, examples, and derivations live on a blackboard or a digital board and projector. I used this approach to engage students in developing the argument during class. The saved handwritten notes and annotations are part of that teaching. Some board work was not recorded, so the available PDFs do not capture every explanation developed in class.

I also adapted courses as students’ needs and the curriculum changed. The archive preserves substantial revisions across offerings, including changes in depth, organization, and delivery during the pandemic. In research seminars, I tailored the readings and discussion to the questions students were developing.

## Assessment design

Designing new problems was a central part of my teaching in these courses. I usually wrote examinations anew for each offering, with occasional reuse or adaptation. The retired exams serve both as resources for learning and as examples of my assessment design: how I asked students to reason through unfamiliar problems, connect ideas, and develop an argument.

The collection also includes worked solutions, grading rubrics, and answer booklets where available. The course pages identify materials developed with reference to textbooks or other instructors’ work.

## General-equilibrium lecture notes

My [general-equilibrium lecture notes](https://github.com/alejandro-melo-ponce/ge-notes) began with ECON498, and I wrote much of the mathematical material while teaching the course. They have since grown to book length and beyond the scope of that course, while retaining their purpose and character as lecture notes. I do not intend to develop them for formal publication as a book.

These notes are intended to guide students, encourage their interest in the subject, and serve as a main reference during the course and in more advanced study. They draw on many sources acknowledged throughout the document; I make no claim to originality in the underlying ideas or results. My contribution lies in the organization, presentation, emphasis, and connections drawn across the material, reflecting my own taste and approach to teaching. In some cases, this also includes my own proofs of established results. I maintain the notes in their own repository and continue to revise and expand them.

## Using the collection

Start with a course README for its scope, teaching context, references, and material index. Within courses, files are organized by offering, topic, or stage of development, with meaningful revisions preserved and shared copies linked where appropriate. The amount of material available differs by course; the indexes show the notes, exercises, solutions, and exams included.

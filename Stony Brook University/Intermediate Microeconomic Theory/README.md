# ECO303 — Intermediate Microeconomic Theory

**Stony Brook University · Undergraduate · Fall 2016 and Spring 2019**  
**Instructor:** Alejandro Melo Ponce

I taught this course in Fall 2016 and Spring 2019. Here I share my syllabi, selected lecture notes, assignments, practice problems, and retired exams from both offerings.

My aim was to introduce students to the tools of microeconomic analysis: how consumers choose under certainty and uncertainty, how firms make decisions subject to technological constraints, and how markets are organized. I used mathematical models alongside applications, emphasizing the economic intuition behind the results. I expected students to have a background in introductory economics and single-variable calculus.

## Textbooks and lecture materials

I used different main textbooks in the two offerings:

- **Fall 2016:** Walter Nicholson and Christopher Snyder, *Microeconomic Theory: Basic Principles and Extensions*, 11th edition, Cengage Learning, 2011.
- **Spring 2019:** Hal R. Varian, *Intermediate Microeconomics: A Modern Approach*, W. W. Norton. I accepted the fifth edition or later and recommended the accompanying *Workouts in Intermediate Microeconomics* for practice.

For Fall 2016, I prepared my own slides and also drew on Nicholson’s textbook slides. From Spring 2019, I share the slides I prepared on preferences, utility, and consumer choice.

I have organized the materials by term, keeping both versions where I revised assignments between offerings.
